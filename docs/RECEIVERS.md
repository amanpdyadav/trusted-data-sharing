# Receivers (sharing scenarios)

The app offers one receiver per sharing scenario in the challenge pack: 27 in `scenarios/`. On the
**Receiver & plan** step it shows only the receivers whose tables are all in the opened data; the others are greyed
out under "Receivers made for other data". Any dataset can also use **Someone else** (describe your own receiver).

## Where they come from

- `HEALTH-S1` and `HEALTH-S2` are hand-written (and `plans/health-s1.yaml` is the tuned HEALTH-S1 plan).
- The other 25 are generated by `python -m evaluation scenarios` from the pack's `metadata/scenarios.json`
  (recipient, purpose, trust level, threat model, legal context, suggested targets). Their **data request** comes from
  the organisers' utility tasks for each scenario: the columns each analysis uses, resolved to their tables, plus the
  ID columns that join those tables.
  - **Row-level receivers** (contractual, controlled research, internal) get `needs` and k ≥ 5.
  - **Public receivers** get `statistics` (counts or means with differential privacy, ε = 1 per release, cap 4),
    because the organisers say no row-level data may be published openly. All tables of one dashboard come from one
    table, since the planner bounds each person's contribution with one key.
  - **Hourly time** only where measurement chose it: CYBER-S1, ENERGY-S2, ENERGY-S3, FIN-S1, FIN-S2 (hourly raised the
    combined score and kept privacy ≥ 0.9). Elsewhere the planner's default resolution for the trust level applies.

Regenerate after changing the generator (keeps HEALTH-S1/S2): `python -m evaluation scenarios --force`.

## Measured result of each receiver (7 Nov)

`make receivers` measures them again and writes `results/receivers.csv`; the web app shows that file on **Release
audit → Receivers benchmark**, with a filter for the receivers that fit the open data.

Each receiver was planned automatically (the rule-based planner, approved for the test), released, and scored with the
organisers' `pets-eval` scorecard. Public dashboards outside health release correctly, but the toolkit only has a
dashboard scorer for the hospital data. "Ready" means combined ≥ 0.70 (our target); "needs tuning" means the automatic
plan breaks some of the receiver's analyses and a person should adjust the plan, as we did for HEALTH-S1. Note that
the HEALTH-S1 row below is the automatic plan (combined 1.00, but it fails the post and news sweep); the tuned
`plans/health-s1.yaml` scores 0.991 and passes all 5 gates.

**Need tuning (12):** the automatic plan keeps privacy high but breaks analyses that need exact times, raw
identifiers or free text. The main causes, from the scorecards:

- **Time too coarse for the analysis:** parking occupancy and dwell time (CITY-S2, CITY-S3), journey chains (MOB-S2).
  Hourly time raises usefulness there but drops privacy to about 0.4, so it needs a different protection (for example
  shift dates per person) rather than finer time.
- **Indicators that must stay verbatim:** threat-intelligence sharing (CYBER-S2) needs attacker IPs and domains
  unchanged; the planner pseudonymises or drops them.
- **Free text needed:** CS-S1, CS-S2, PUB-S2, PUB-S3 analyse texts, which need the text redactor in the plan.
- **Models trained on the protected data:** FIN-S1, FIN-S2, EDU-S2 lose signal from generalised amounts and times.

Each is a plan to tune with `python -m evaluation experiment …` (see `RUNBOOK.md`, section 7), as HEALTH-S1 was.
