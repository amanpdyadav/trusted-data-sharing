We play the attacker. Using only the public knowledge shipped with the challenge, we try to find patients in the hospital data, and we link the hospital and bank datasets to each other. This becomes the “before” moment of our demo.

The headline: linking two datasets

A hospital and a bank both publish data with names removed. An attacker joins them on just birth date and postcode.

Each link joins a person's emergency-department diagnosis with their income band and occupation from the bank. Neither organisation published anything that looks identifying on its own. This is the mosaic effect that TrustED and the challenge's threat model warn about.

The quickstart's protection (age bands, hourly times, 3-character diagnoses) helps against news stories but does nothing against social media posts. Our target is 0% on every attacker while keeping the research tasks working.

What the attacker sees

A news story: the rescue service got a call at 8 on Monday about an accident in Turku. A woman of about 90 fell at home and was taken to hospital by ambulance.
Names removed: 7 matching visits · Quickstart: 19 · the attacker cannot tell which
A news story: a named ice-hockey player was taken to the Aurajoki emergency department on 17 May 2025.
Names removed: exactly 1 matching visit · diagnosis revealed (R07.4, chest pain)
Quickstart: still exactly 1 match · diagnosis still revealed (R07)

The second story is the one to show in the demo: even after the toolkit's example protection, one news article is enough to learn a named person's diagnosis. When the exact date and a rare combination stay in the data, generalising other columns doesn't help. Our per-patient date shifting is designed to break exactly this.

How it fits the demo

  1. Before: show the mosaic link and the hockey-player match on names-removed data.
  2. Protect: run our tool on the same data for HEALTH-S1.
  3. After: rerun the same attacks on our release. The attack page picks it up automatically from outputs/health/.
  4. Keep the value: show the utility tasks still working.

Every person in these examples is synthetic. The attack uses only the attacker files and ground truth provided in the challenge pack.
