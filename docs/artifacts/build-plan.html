PETs Transformer build plan

A privacy tool that finds what is sensitive in a dataset, protects it in proportion to who receives it, and proves with measured numbers that the protected data is still useful.

Submissions close Sun 8 Nov, 10:00 Company Q&A Sat 09:00–12:00 · 14:30–18:00 Prizes €2,000 / €750 / €250

What the challenge asks for

One working prototype that, for a synthetic dataset and a defined sharing scenario:

  1. Detects what each column and free-text field contains: direct identifiers, quasi-identifiers, sensitive attributes, technical IDs, free text.
  2. Selects a proportionate privacy technique per field, justified by the scenario: who gets the data, why, and what an attacker knows.
  3. Transforms the data: pseudonymise, generalise, suppress, redact, add differential-privacy noise or synthesise.
  4. Demonstrates before and after: re-identification risk down, analytical value kept, remaining risks stated honestly.

Judges score technical implementation, innovation, impact (measured risk reduction with utility kept) and presentation. The submission is the prototype, a short demo video in English, a description and the repository.

What we were given

10 synthetic datasets

One fake population of 100,000 people in Southwest Finland across health, mobility, smart city, energy, finance, cyber, education, public services and customer service, plus an unlabelled HR export.

27 sharing scenarios

Three per dataset. Each names the recipient, purpose, threat model, utility tasks and suggested privacy targets.

Ground truth and attackers

Column labels, 354,000 labelled PII spans in free text, attacker background knowledge and holdout records, so privacy is measured rather than claimed.

The pets-eval toolkit

Scores detection, redaction, k-anonymity, linkage attacks and utility tasks, and produces an HTML before/after scorecard. Our measurement layer comes ready-made.

What we build: a privacy engineer in a box

Most teams will write one-off scripts for one dataset. We build the tool a data-sharing team would actually use: upload a dataset, choose a scenario, and get a justified, measured, protected release.

STEP 1

Detect

Rules plus an LLM classify every column and find PII in free text.

STEP 2

Plan

A technique per field with a plain-language reason. The user can override it.

STEP 3

Transform

Deterministic code: keyed pseudonyms, bands, suppression, redaction, DP noise.

STEP 4

Measure

pets-eval scorecard: risk and utility, before and after.

STEP 5

Trust report + manifest

A plain-language report and a verifiable record of what was released, for whom and why.

AI only where it adds value. The LLM classifies columns, finds personal details in messy text and writes explanations. Plain code does the protecting, which keeps results reliable and defensible.

A visible trade-off. A privacy-versus-utility view per scenario shows the scores move as the user changes a decision. That is our innovation and presentation story.

Three additions from the sponsor research

TrustED's own work centres on data minimisation, transparency and tools that non-experts can trust. These three additions make that alignment obvious to the judges.

1 · Data minimisation framing

We pitch the tool as data minimisation for datasets: share only what the scenario's purpose needs, at the resolution it needs. Every PET decision in the app cites that purpose, for example “arrival time rounded to the week, because the revisit analysis only needs weekly patterns”. No extra build work.

2 · Release manifest

Every protected release ships with manifest.json and a readable page: recipient, purpose, the transformation and parameters per column, before/after scores, remaining risks and a SHA-256 fingerprint of each output file. Generated automatically. Owner: Transform, with UI rendering it. About half a day.

3 · Trust report for non-experts

Written for a hospital data manager, not a cryptographer: how many people could be identified before and after, how well the analysis still works, and what risks remain, all in plain sentences with the real scorecard numbers. Owner: Story and submission, using the LLM to draft from the scorecard.

Rules we must not break

  • Never publish rowmap_*.csv files. They exist only for scoring.
  • Every privacy claim states its method and parameters, for example “k ≥ 5 on age band, sex, municipality and arrival week” or “ε = 1, person-level, at most 4 visits per person”.
  • Use only the provided synthetic data, and disclose remaining risks.

The challenge pack is for our team only. Keep this page and the repository private until the organisers say otherwise.
