The key insight: minimisation beat generalisation

We tried three versions. Keeping sex and arrival mode and coarsening them only where groups were small gave utility 0.93–0.97. Dropping them entirely, because no HEALTH-S1 question uses them, gave 0.98: with fewer attributes to protect, far fewer visits had to lose their municipality (1,078 instead of 8,136). Data minimisation isn't only a legal principle here; it measurably keeps more of the data that matters.

The other big move was the timestamp. Attackers match on the arrival date; the research needs only the hour. Releasing the hour alone took small groups from 83% to 18% before any other step.

Checks we ran

  • k-anonymity: every combination of age band, municipality and arrival hour is shared by at least 5 visits (median 56).
  • l-diversity: every group contains at least 2 different diagnosis groups, so no group reveals one diagnosis for everyone in it.
  • Attacks: the news, social-media and directory attackers re-identify 0 of their targets. The ice-hockey player from the attack demo can no longer be found.
  • Residual identifier scan: the toolkit's scan finds no ID codes, phones, cards or emails in the released files.
  • Release manifest: manifest.json records the scenario, each decision with its reason, the scores, and a SHA-256 fingerprint of each released file.

Along the way the scan flagged our own pseudonyms: some random tokens happened to look like Finnish phone numbers or pass the card-number checksum. We now prefix every token with letters (pt…, v…, lab…) so released data never looks like an identifier.

Remaining risks, stated honestly

  • The organisers hold stronger hidden attackers; these results cover only the public ones.
  • The patient pseudonym links all visits of one person (up to 28). A patient's sequence of visits could be distinctive to someone who knows their history.
  • Pseudonymised data is still personal data under GDPR. This release is designed for a controlled research environment, not open publication.
  • Free text is not released yet: the complaint field is dropped and clinical notes are withheld until our redactor is ready.
  • These are self-assessment scores from the toolkit, not the official judging score.
