Starting point: the toolkit's quickstart on HEALTH-S1

The quickstart applies simple protection to the emergency-department visits: keyed patient pseudonyms, 10-year age bands, arrival time rounded to the hour, 3-character diagnosis codes and basic regex redaction. Then it scores the result. These are self-assessment scores, not the official judging score.

Where the risk comes from

Risk componentScoreWhat it means
Records with k < 50.9999% of visits are in groups of fewer than 5 people who share the same quasi-identifiers. This is the main problem to fix.
Linkage re-identification0.055The attackers (news clips, social posts, public directory) single out about 5.5% of their targets.
Direct identifiers retained0.00No names, ID codes or phone numbers left.
Free-text identifier leakage0.00Only for the short complaint field; clinical notes were not released here.

Before any protection, 98.9% of patients are unique on birth date, sex and postcode alone. Removing names does not make this data anonymous.

What we have to do to win

  • Fix k < 5 without killing utility. The baseline that forces k ≥ 5 drops utility to 0.40. Our plan: split arrival time into hour-of-day plus coarse dates, use per-patient date shifting, and generalise only the columns the attackers use.
  • Release the lab results with a consistent visit pseudonym so H-U7 becomes answerable.
  • Release the clinical notes, properly redacted. That's where our LLM redactor shows its value.
